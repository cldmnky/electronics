# Printed parts: NAV keytop, value knob, B3F button cap — and the inventory

Three 3D-printed parts for the MIDI Brain front panel, generated from
parametric Python: an **NAV keytop** for the ALPS Alpine SKRHABE010 5-way
switch (U5), a **value knob** for the Bourns PEC11R-4015F-S0024 encoder (U6),
and a **B3F button cap** printed seven times for the Omron B3F-4055 switches
(SW1–SW7).

The printed B3F cap is the **standard** key top; a bought Omron/ARATAS
B32-13x0 is the documented alternative. This is a panel accessory, so it has no
JLCPCB/SMT BOM row: the seven switches stay on the generated hand BOM and the
caps are printed from `button-cap.stl`.

Status: **PROVISIONAL printed parts, not released drawings.** Every fit number
below is either sourced (datasheet / `lib/layout.ts` / `mechanical/layout.json`)
or an explicit ASSUMPTION that the first article has to measure. Nothing here is
an acceptance measurement, and nothing here changes a PCB or panel rule.

### DO NOT PRINT / DO NOT USE FOR FIT: the two board mockups

`mockup-front-board.stl` and `mockup-rear-board.stl` (item 7) are generated from
`mechanical/layout.json`, and the cuts `step_assembly.py` applies are **not** the
cuts the current PCB sources carry. The slab outlines and Z planes are current;
the cuts are not. Neither file may be printed, laid over a real board or part, or
used to settle any fit or clearance question.

- `mockup-front-board.stl`: cuts the obsolete `notchBounds` rectangle (x −55…50,
  y 48…56) instead of the U-shaped display opening; drills **four Ø4.3 mm** holes
  at the retired LCD carrier positions (Ø4.3 is the display stack's hole size,
  and the current front board has **no hole there at all** — the carrier moved to
  the rear board), the four old panel-standoff holes at (−61, −53), (61, −53),
  (−61, 53) and (57, 41), and the two feedthrough-bracket holes at (−66.5, 53)
  and (55, 53) — **none of which the current board has**. It does drill the four
  front↔rear spacer holes and the four `PANEL_MOUNT_POINTS` M3 holes.
- `mockup-rear-board.stl`: **lacks** the four **Ø2.7 mm** LCD carrier holes
  (`LCD_MOUNT_HOLES`) that the current rear board carries. It does drill the four
  front↔rear spacer holes.

Unaffected: `mockup-panel.stl` (validated against the canonical 20 apertures +
14 drills, still a print check and not a fit), `mockup-pi-panel.stl`,
`scale-check-100mm.stl` and `light-pipe-reach-gauge.stl`. The repair is one
tracked work item in `docs/TODO.md`; it is deliberately **not** attempted
partially, because a half-repaired mockup would look current and not be.

**Item 5 (the feedthrough / strain-relief bracket) is an INSTALLED module part**, not a
print aid: it is intended to sit on the front board's rear face and carry the
feedthrough cable tails. Its fastening and retention are **unapproved** (its
dedicated board screw holes are removed from `front.circuit.tsx`) and its printed
fit is unmeasured. Items 7–9 are PRINT AIDS: nothing in them is a fit approval,
and the mockups reproduce the model's own geometry.

The **display carrier is the one printed part this review could not improve**: its
outline depends on where the module's two outboard boss points can be carried,
which is unresolved, so drawing it now would invent geometry. Its dependencies are
the module seating, the DSI route and the rear-board hole pattern, all open.

**Retention language is deliberate everywhere in this directory**: there is no
snap, no detent and no proven grip in any part. The words to look for are
`nominal clearance`, `ASSUMPTION`, `PROVISIONAL`, `BLOCKER` and `unverified`.

## How to build, test and regenerate

```bash
# CadQuery is NOT in the system python; use a local venv (never the system one)
uv venv /tmp/cq-venv
uv pip install --python /tmp/cq-venv/bin/python cadquery

# regenerate the artefacts (each run rebuilds, self-validates, exports and re-reads)
/tmp/cq-venv/bin/python mechanical/printed/nav_keytop.py
/tmp/cq-venv/bin/python mechanical/printed/knob.py
/tmp/cq-venv/bin/python mechanical/printed/button_cap.py

# the four button-cap fit specimens (3.80 / 3.85 / 3.90 / 4.00 mm locator recess)
/tmp/cq-venv/bin/python mechanical/printed/button_cap.py --specimens

# the NAV motion analysis: standard vs the experimental variant
/tmp/cq-venv/bin/python mechanical/printed/nav_fit.py

# tests: the pure-geometry and STL checks run anywhere, the rebuild tests need the venv
cd mechanical/printed && python3 -m unittest test_printed_parts test_nav_fit -v
cd mechanical/printed && /tmp/cq-venv/bin/python -m unittest test_printed_parts test_nav_fit -v
```

`make mechanical` (and therefore `make test`) runs the standalone
`test_printed_parts` suite; the CadQuery-dependent rebuild tests skip cleanly
when CadQuery is absent, the pure geometry and committed-STL checks still run.

The six generated artefacts are committed even though the repository-wide
`.gitignore` excludes `*.stl` and `*.step`: the test suite re-reads these exact
files, so a checkout without them cannot run the artefact and rebuild checks.
They were added with `git add -f`; regenerating them rewrites the files (the STEP
header carries a creation timestamp), and the tests compare geometry, not bytes.

### Why the head is the same diameter as the neck

A one-piece panel cannot pass a part that is wider than the aperture at both
ends. The cap is therefore dropped through the 7.2 mm hole from the **rear**
(the head leads, 0.10 mm per side), and the flange - the last feature to enter -
stays outside and bears on the panel rear face.

### Assembly order (required)

1. Press the cap through the NAV aperture from the **rear** of the panel: head
   first. The flange stops it against the panel rear face; the head now stands
   4 mm proud of the panel front.
2. Lower the panel onto the front-board standoffs. The socket mouth leads over
   the switch stem, the stem enters the 1.5 mm socket, and the flange comes to
   rest on the panel rear.

What keeps the cap in place is **unproven**. The flange gives a forward stop
against the panel rear while the panel is fitted; rearward, the socket floor
reaches the stem top only after the 0.15 mm free play plus the switch travel. No
claim is made that the cap cannot come off, that it cannot rock, or that the
1.6 mm aperture stops the stem from seeing a bending moment: the tip is a rounded
square in a circular bore and all of those are first-article measurements.

Why those numbers, and what they are based on:

* the tip is `1.95 ±0.05` across flats with `R0.5` corners, and the vendor model's
  slices put its farthest extent at **±1.184 mm** between Z 3.5 and Z 4.9 above the
  mounting face, with the **Ø3 circular section lower, at Z 2.5**;
* corner radius **R0.60** clears the tip's R0.5 corners by 0.10 mm — a 0.5 mm
  corner on both parts would need an exact match to slide, which no FDM print
  gives, and anything under R0.4 is a hairline in a 0.4 mm nozzle. The four
  specimens therefore differ ONLY in the across-flats size;
* socket depth **1.40 mm** with the same 0.15 mm free play, so the bore spans
  **3.75…5.15 mm above the mounting face** — it covers the square tip band and
  **stops above the Ø3 collar at 2.5 mm**: `only_engages_the_square_tip = true`,
  `reaches_the_circular_collar = false`. The validator refuses any specimen whose
  mouth would swallow the collar;
* the wall from the socket corner to the D5.60 shank is **1.49…1.63 mm**. Printed
  mouth-down the **blind square socket roof is still a BRIDGE** over the bore, and
  the flange overhang is the same unsupported cantilever as the standard part:
  these prototypes are **not** a support-free print;
* the **ROCK variant** narrows the neck to D6.80, i.e. 0.20 mm per side in the
  unchanged 7.2 mm aperture, which raises the tilt before binding from 7.1° to
  14.0°. It is a separate specimen because that also costs guidance and lets the
  cap slide further sideways: **neither is recommended, and neither is the
  installed part.**

What the prototypes do NOT establish: that the socket grips or retains the cap,
that it centres it, that the cap cannot rock or press the switch, or that the
square/radius/rotation match the supplied switch. The tip shape is **vendor MODEL
geometry**, not a measured tolerance, and the `0.49` "Center of stem rotation"
label's endpoints are unresolved. Choose the size by MEASUREMENT:

| trial, on the real switch | record |
|---|---|
| measure the real tip across flats and at its corners | actual vs 1.95 ±0.05 / ±1.184 |
| measure its rotation and where the flat starts | degrees, and the height above the PCB |
| press the prototype on and lift it off | does it stay, or come away with the hand? |
| rock it in four directions at the head | does it tilt freely, bind, or lift the flange off the panel? |

| measure engagement depth | actual socket contact length on the real tip |

### Part 1b: the NAV motion model and the experimental variant

The owner asked whether the joystick cap design can be improved. Answering that
needs the motion, not the shape, so `nav_fit.py` models it. All of the following
are **parameters with ranges, never constants**, because none of them is in the
ALPS catalog or has been measured: stem tilt (0–1°), stem lateral movement
(0–0.15 mm), where the stem's movement pivots (0–2 mm), the printed neck's radial
error (0–0.10 mm) and the FR-4 hole's radial error (0–0.10 mm).

What the model reports for the **standard part** (all four lateral directions and
the centre push; the tests run all of them):

* neck clearance 0.10 mm per side at both panel faces;
* the tilt at which both faces bind: `atan((cl_front + cl_rear)/1.6)` = **7.1°**;
* nominal stem tilt and lateral movement: **no binding**;
* at the bound (0.15 mm stem lateral movement): **BINDS** — the cap can follow
  only by jamming the neck in the hole. That is a finding, not a defect to tune
  away: nothing has measured the stem's real movement;
* centre push: the 0.15 mm free play equals the 0.15 mm centre travel, so a push
  closes the free play before the switch moves (unchanged from before).

The **variant** (`variant_profile`: two 0.40 mm lands at D7.00 at the two panel
faces, a 0.80 mm waist at D6.40 between them, a 0.50 mm lead-in at the head) is
analysed, not built, and is clearly **EXPERIMENTAL — not the standard**:

* its lands keep the standard diameter, so the guidance and the 7.1° tilt limit
  are **unchanged** — the variant buys nothing there;
* it relieves the middle 0.80 mm by 0.30 mm radially, so **one local 0.15 mm
  radial defect at mid-panel JAMS the standard part and passes the variant**.
  That is the measurable improvement, and it matters because an FDM print's
  tightest material is often at one face while a routed FR-4 hole can be
  undersize anywhere along its length;
* it does **not** improve lateral following, does not grip the stem, and has not
  been printed. No STL exists for it, on purpose: it must not be mistaken for a
  released part.

Neither part can be called a retention feature. The only axial retention in front
of the flange is the stem fit in the socket, and whether it holds is a
first-article check.

### Encoder blockers — the knob geometry is gated

The sourced page-2/page-3 numbers do not support the stack the knob was designed
against. Three findings, all reported by `validate_knob()` and `knob.py` as
`BLOCKER` notices. **No encoder, PCB, panel or knob geometry was changed to hide
them**, and the encoder stays in the BOM.

1. **The panel cannot be nutted on this encoder.** The body/bushing shoulder is
   6.5 mm above the PCB and `LB` is 5.0 mm, so the threaded bushing ends 11.5 mm
   above the PCB = **0.90 mm past the 10.6 mm panel front**. The supplied nut
   (2.0) plus washer (0.5) need **2.50 mm**. Shortfall **1.60 mm**; with every
   recorded tolerance at its worst it is **2.70 mm** (the shoulder and `LB` each
   take the ±0.3 mm general tolerance). This is a design incompatibility, not a
   fit tolerance.
   *Candidate only, NOT selected, NOT in the BOM*: Bourns
   `PEC11R-4020F-S0024` (L 20 with LB 7) would give 2.9 mm past the panel front,
   i.e. **0.40 mm nominal** beyond the 2.50 mm stack. A worst case that carries
   EVERY recorded term — shoulder 6.5 ±0.3, `LB` 7.0 ±0.3, nut 2.0 +0.2, washer
   0.5 +0.3 — gives **2.3 mm of thread against 3.0 mm of hardware, i.e. −0.70 mm**.
   The panel-thickness tolerance is not recorded and is excluded, so this is a
   bound of the known terms, not a guaranteed clearance. Its
   LCSC lead is `C143788` (0 detents, 24 pulses; a temporary snapshot showed 39
   units, which is not a stock guarantee). **No main-shop SKU is VERIFIED**: an
   exact Electrokit query returned no products, digikey.se returned HTTP 403 and a
   Conrad manual search was not completed, so the component library's
   `preferred`/`alternate` gate (exact CAD + main-shop SKU) cannot pass today. It stays a lead until the owner approves it AND the gate passes.
2. **The D-bore cannot pass the round part of the shaft.** The flat starts
   `L − F` = 8.0 mm from the shoulder, i.e. **14.5 mm above the PCB**, and the knob
   rear face is at **13.3 mm** above the PCB. So **1.20 mm of ROUND Ø6.0 shaft**
   has to pass the D-shaped section of the bore, whose flat is 1.60 mm from the
   axis while the shaft's round radius is 3.00 mm: **1.40 mm radial
   interference**. As drawn, the part cannot be assembled onto the shaft.
   Remedies are the owner's decision (a round bore mouth deeper than 1.2 mm, a
   longer flat, an L 20 shaft, or a knob that sits further forward). None is
   applied.
3. **The full centre-push travel is not guaranteed.** The knob rear face is
   0.20 mm above the supplied nut + washer nominally (−0.30 mm worst case), and
   the encoder's centre travel is 0.50 ±0.30 mm: nominal shortfall **0.30 mm**,
   worst case **0.80 mm**. This does **not** say the switch will or will not
   actuate — the actuation position and the real hardware tolerances are
   unmeasured.

The parameterised model (`printed_geometry.encoder_stack()`) runs the whole stack
for **L 15 and L 20** and for any bore/flat plane, so these numbers can be
re-computed when a measurement replaces an input instead of being re-typed.

## Part 3: B3F-4055 button cap (`button-cap.stl`), 7 off

**The bought B32 alternative does not suit this stack, and the reason is now
verified**: Omron's `10.0 ±0.4` is PCB top → **cap top** and `1.0–2.0` is the cap

### Assembly order (required)

1. Print seven caps (see the print settings below).
2. Solder SW1–SW7 and clean the plungers.
3. Press a cap's 3.85 mm locator recess over each 3.80 ±0.1 mm plunger: the
   recess floor must land flat on the plunger face. Check that the cap sits
   straight and does not rock.
4. Lower the panel. Each plate enters its 12.25 mm opening with 0.225 mm per
   side; the cap top stands 0.80 mm proud of the panel front. The aperture
   centres the cap; there is no other guide.

The cap has **no snap and no skirt**, and the 3.85 mm recess on a 3.80 ±0.1 mm
plunger is **nominal clearance (0.025 mm per side), not a snap and not a
retaining feature**: there is no interference, no detent and no printed spring.
Axial retention is therefore the printed fit on the plunger alone, and FDM holes
usually print undersize, so whether a cap stays on when the panel is lifted is
**UNKNOWN until measured**. Do not add a skirt — the 12.25 mm cutout cannot
accept one. Do not claim a snap.

**Fit specimens** (`fit-specimens/`, four locator sizes 3.80 / 3.85 / 3.90 /
4.00 mm, everything else identical) exist so that this is decided by
measurement: print all four, measure the real plunger, and record the result.
`fit-specimens/README.md` holds the result table and the pre-load / retention
tests to run on SW1–SW7.

**Fit trials to run with each specimen, on every one of SW1–SW7** (fill in the

## Print orientation and material

All three parts print **in one piece, axis vertical — but NOT all without
supports**, and none of that is proven on a printer. Each part needs its
slicer/print check:

**NAV keytop: socket mouth DOWN** (the D5.60/D3.20 annular face on the build
plate, the head at the top). **The slicer opens the model FRONT FACE DOWN, so
rotate the part 180° to reach this setup** - do not let the slicer choose.

This orientation does **NOT** print without support, and the earlier
"no supports / bridged from the shank" wording was wrong:

* the flange starts at Z 2.80 with r 4.80 while the shank below it is r 2.80, so
  its first layer is an **unsupported annular CANTILEVER of 2.00 mm radial width**
  (1.40 mm beyond the 45° rear chamfer ring). It is supported on ONE side only,
  so it is not a bridge;
* the blind socket roof (Ø3.20 in the standard part, square in the prototypes)
  **is** a bridge: it closes a hole with no material underneath;
* the bed contact is a 1.20 mm wide annulus (about 17 mm^2) - **use a 4 mm brim**
  and keep the first layer slow.

Plan for it: enable **support under the flange**, or print head-down with the
flange on the bed and accept a worse finger face, then **check the result in the
slicer preview before printing**. The flange's bearing face and the socket roof
will sag otherwise. That is a first-article print-plan item, not a proven
process.

**Value knob: rear face DOWN** (the face with the bore mouth chamfer on the
plate, the front face at the top).

* every wall is vertical: the bore, the D-flat chord and the 16 flutes;
* no overhang anywhere; both end chamfers are 45 deg;
* the bore's mouth chamfer opens onto the plate, so the lead-in is clean;
* the front face is the last layer (visible, flat);
* the only bridge is the D2.50 grub pilot through the 3.65 mm wall; its top
  sags slightly, which is why it is **tapped M3 afterwards**.

**B3F button cap: rear face DOWN** (the locator recess opens onto the build
plate, the finger face at the top).

* every wall is vertical: the 11.80 square outline and the 3.85 square recess;
* no overhang and no support; the only bridge is the 3.85 mm square recess roof
  at 0.6 mm, and it is short;
* the finger face is the flat top layer (visible);
* the bed contact is an 11.80 mm square with a 3.85 mm square hole - **use a
  4 mm brim** and keep the first layer slow;
* **print solid** (100% infill, or 4+ perimeters): the part is only 4.70 mm tall
  and a press fit must not crush a sparse interior.

**Material: PETG** (0.4 mm nozzle, 0.15-0.2 mm layers, 3 perimeters). PETG
because (a) the knob is clamped by an M3 set screw - PLA creeps and cracks at a
tapped hole, PETG is tougher and slightly compliant; (b) the keytop is a push fit
on a plastic stem and takes an impatient thumb, so a slightly compliant,
non-brittle material is the safe choice; (c) matte black PETG matches the panel.
Use ASA/ABS if the module will run warm for long; PLA is acceptable for a
fit-check prototype only.

## The first article must measure

NAV keytop:

1. the panel aperture (nominal 7.20) and the printed neck (nominal 7.00): the
   head and neck must slide in with 0.10 mm per side.
2. the printed socket bore (nominal 3.20) on the REAL stem tip: measure the tip
   across flats and at its corners first, because the vendor model shows a
   **rounded square** (1.95 ±0.05, R0.5, ±1.184 mm) and not a Ø3 cylinder, so the
   radial play is 0.10…0.62 mm per side. **No friction fit and no retention is
   assumed**; record whether the cap stays on and whether it rocks. The ALPS page
   also says, under notes common to the series, "Do not apply a force from the
   side of the stem" - ask ALPS, or check on the sample, whether that restricts
   pushing a 4-way cap sideways.
3. the socket depth (1.50, limit 1.70) against the real stem, and the switch's
   moving collar above the 1.7 mm fitting area (not dimensioned in the catalog):
   nothing but the stem top may touch the socket floor.
4. the stem top height (nominal 5.00 above the board, so Z 5.00) and the
   resulting axial free play with the flange on the panel rear: target 0.15 mm,
   band 0.10-0.20 mm. Pre-load shows up as a switch that clicks without travel.
5. whether the cap stays on the stem when the panel is lifted off.
6. side-load feel: pushing the head at an angle must not tilt the stem (the
   1.6 mm collar in the aperture is what should take it).

Value knob:

1. the supplied nut + washer stack (assumed 2.50 mm). Everything axial follows
   it: with a thicker stack the knob must sit further forward and the 15.0 mm
   shaft tip can stand proud of a 16 mm knob.
2. the real shaft length `L`, the real threaded-bushing length `LB` and the real
   shaft diameter at the flat. The model currently uses the sourced page-3
   values (L 15 from the shoulder, LB 5.0, flat plane 1.50 from the axis) but
   every one of them must be checked on the supplied part, because a different
   `LB` changes the nut problem and a different flat position changes whether the
   bore can pass the round section at all.
3. the shaft flat's clocking. The bore flat and the grub screw are both at 0 deg
   (+X); if the encoder is clocked differently, change `KNOB_FLAT_ANGLE_DEG` and
   regenerate - do not rework the bore.
4. the printed bore fit and the tapped M3 pilot: does the set screw clamp the
   shaft without stripping the plastic, and does the knob run without wobble on
   the D-flat.
5. the assembled clearance to the display opening (planned 2.50 mm) - a plan
   view check; the knob itself is entirely in front of the panel.
6. rotational feel: no wobble, no axial end-float beyond the grub screw's play.

B3F button cap (7 off):

1. the plate in the real 12.25 mm routed opening: target 0.225 mm per side; no
   binding, no rattle.
2. the printed locator (nominal 3.85) on the real 3.80 ±0.1 mm plunger: it must
   push on, sit flat, and stay on when the panel is lifted. Measure the actual
   plunger and recess; FDM holes usually print undersize.
